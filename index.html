THE LOOP

Every step leaves one artifact.

  1. Brief

    Define the problem, audience, limits and assessment criteria together with the partner.

    ARTIFACT 01 / 05

    A district NGO wants more secondary students to use its evening study rooms. Attendance dropped 40% since 2024.

    Challenge Brief

    Audience
    Secondary 3 to 5 students and their parents
    Limits
    No access to personal data. Public statistics and own interviews only
    Effort
    About 12 hours over four weeks

  2. Build

    Research, interviews, bilingual comms, AI collaboration and a first prototype.

    ARTIFACT 02 / 05

    Where the drop actually happens, drawn from three interviews and two months of attendance data.

    Problem Map

    Method
    Stakeholder interviews, public statistics, service journey
    AI use
    Prompts, sources and version history logged
    Output
    Problem map, research summary, first draft

  3. Feedback

    The partner and beneficiaries respond at midpoint and again at final review.

    ARTIFACT 03 / 05

    The partner responds at midpoint and again at final review, in writing.

    Partner Feedback

    Kept
    Evening slot analysis is usable as-is
    Pushed back
    Volunteer capacity was overestimated
    Risk
    Proposal assumes a room already booked twice a week

  4. Refine

    Compare the feedback, revise, and explain every trade-off and judgement call.

    ARTIFACT 04 / 05

    What changed after feedback, and why each call was made.

    Revision & Decision Log

    Kept
    Opening moved to 18:00, per three interviews and attendance data
    Dropped
    AI suggested a points reward. Conflicts with the non-competition rule
    Risk
    Interview sample skews to current users, missing those who left

  5. Verify

    Mentor and partner verify against the rubric. The pack is sealed, a badge is issued.

    ARTIFACT 05 / 05

    Mentor and partner verify the work against the rubric. The pack is sealed and a badge is issued.

    Evidence Pack

    Verified
    Problem definition, responsible AI use, revision quality
    Issued
    Open Badges 3.0 credential, criteria and evidence attached
    Portable
    Stays with the student, readable by anyone, revocable

ABOUT

WHY WE EXIST

Ability is hard to see.

Projects, clubs, volunteering, part-time work and personal making are scattered and carry no format an outsider can read. Universities and employers end up looking at scores, résumé lines and self-description. TOIVO exists to turn what a young person actually did into evidence someone else can verify.

TOIVO is Finnish for hope. 托禾 reads as to hold up, and what grows. The company name carries both.

INSIGHTS

What we have researched.

We have not delivered a project yet. What follows is the research behind the method: what we read, tested and wrote down while specifying it. Each entry names the question, the sources, and what we concluded.

2026-08Interaction

Where the fold should fall

A departure-board flip cuts a word in half. Cutting a card title in half instead produces a readable but wrong pairing. The seam belongs where the card’s structure already breaks.

2026-08Colour

Why a warm grey, and only one

Refactoring UI ships 24 palettes and 30 colour families. Only one neutral ramp is warm. Everything else fights an olive brand colour at 145 degrees of hue separation.

2026-07Typography

Traditional Chinese on the web, at size

A full Hong Kong-form CJK face is 10.3MB. Subsetting takes it to 121KB. The engine defaults for text-autospace diverge, so mixed Latin and Chinese spacing must be set explicitly.

2026-07Assessment

What a rubric can and cannot see

Scoring a prototype rewards polish. Scoring the revision log rewards judgement. We chose the second, which means the artifact set has to capture decisions as well as outputs.

Body text meets 4.5:1 across the site, apart from the brief home-page mouse effect described below.
